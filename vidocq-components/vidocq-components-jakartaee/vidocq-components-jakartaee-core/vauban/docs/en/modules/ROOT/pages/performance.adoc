= Performance
:description: What Vauban costs at run time — qualifier resolution, injection, events, interception, client proxies and the start — and how it is measured.

[.lead]
Vauban does at build time what a runtime container does on every lookup, so the numbers that matter
are the ones on the hot path: resolving a qualified injection point, a programmatic lookup, firing a
qualified event, an intercepted call and a call through a client proxy. *Every run, with its raw output and its caveats*, is kept
in link:{repo-vidocq}/src/branch/main/vidocq-components/vidocq-components-jakartaee/vidocq-components-jakartaee-core/vauban/BENCH.md[BENCH.md] — this page is the reading of it, and each figure names its entry.

== TL;DR

The latest runs of the container's hot path are BENCH-20261028-02 and BENCH-20261028-07, measured on
2026-10-28. Against the reference taken before vauban#70 (BENCH-20260914-01, 2026-09-14), same
benchmark (`QualifierResolutionBenchmark`), same machine:

[cols="2,2,2,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Benchmark | 2026-09-14, before vauban#70 | 2026-10-28 | time | allocation

| `dependentCreation`
| 41561 ns, 65245 B
| 10449 ns, 36184 B (BENCH-20261028-02)
| *3.98× faster*
| *−45 %*

| `programmaticLookup`
| 6590 ns, 11691 B
| 1189 ns, 7075 B (BENCH-20261028-02)
| *5.54× faster*
| *−39 %*

| `interceptedCall`
| 651 ns, 3251 B
| 14.1 ns, 96 B (BENCH-20261028-07)
| *46× faster*
| *−97 %*

| `qualifiedEvent`
| 4673 ns, 6941 B
| 3576 ns, 7861 B (BENCH-20261028-02)
| 1.31× faster
| +13 %
|===

*Pragmatic read*: creating a bean with qualified injection points and looking one up programmatically
— the two operations an application performs constantly — cost a quarter and a fifth of what they did,
for roughly half the garbage. An intercepted call is now a few nanoseconds and 96 bytes.

Two campaigns did this: vauban#70 (September) stopped reading annotations at run time, and vauban#227
(October) took the reflection out of the generated code. The table spans both. BENCH.md attributes to
each only what it measured on its own: between the end of vauban#70 (BENCH-20260914-04) and
BENCH-20261028-02, `dependentCreation`, `programmaticLookup` and `qualifiedEvent` moved by −23 %, −36 %
and −21 %, with six weeks of `main` in between, and those deltas are recorded, not claimed for vauban#227.

`qualifiedEvent` still allocates more than before vauban#70, and the table says so. See <<what-did-not-move>>.

== Where the time went

=== Qualifier resolution and interceptor bindings (vauban#70)

Three things were removed, in this order. Their effect, measured at the end of the campaign
(BENCH-20260914-04, 2026-09-14), was `dependentCreation` 3.05× faster, `programmaticLookup` 3.53×,
`interceptedCall` 1.81× with 76 % less allocation.

Matching on keys::
Resolution used to rebuild every candidate bean's qualifiers as `java.lang.reflect.Proxy` instances
and compare them member by member with `Method.invoke`, on every lookup. A bean's qualifiers are now
normalized once into an `AnnotationKey` — member defaults applied, `@Nonbinding` members left out —
and matching compares those. This is most of the gain on `dependentCreation` and
`programmaticLookup`.

Injection reading the index::
What remained after that was the *injection point* side: the qualifiers of a field or a parameter
were read off the member on every creation. They come from the descriptor the index built instead.

The interceptor chain worked out once::
The generated subclass asks for its interceptor chain on every call, and the container answered by
walking the class hierarchy, resolving the method reflectively and re-reading every binding
annotation. Which interceptors apply depends on declarations alone, so it is computed once per
(bean class, method).

=== Generated code (vauban#227)

`GeneratedCodeBenchmark` measures the classes Vauban generates, one at a time: `plainCall` a
`@Dependent` bean with nothing around it (the floor), `clientProxyCall` an `@ApplicationScoped` bean
through its client proxy, `interceptedCall` a `@Dependent` bean through its intercepted subclass,
`proxiedInterceptedCall` both, and the creation of an intercepted bean, by `new` or emitted as bytecode.
From the reference before the campaign (BENCH-20261028-01, and BENCH-20261028-05 for the bytecode
creation) to the latest run (BENCH-20261028-07, `main` f6c809c4), all on 2026-10-28:

[cols="2,2,2,1",options="header"]
|===
| Benchmark | before vauban#227 | BENCH-20261028-07 | time

| `plainCall` | 0.34 ns | 0.31 ns | flat
| `clientProxyCall` | 276 ns, 0 B | 5.03 ns, 0 B | *55× faster*
| `interceptedCall` | 84.4 ns, 768 B | 12.1 ns, 96 B | *7× faster*
| `proxiedInterceptedCall` | 382 ns, 768 B | 14.2 ns, 96 B | *27× faster*
| `interceptedCreation` | 3540 ns, 7630 B | 2487 ns, 7845 B | −30 %
| `bytecodeInterceptedCreation` | 3624 ns, 9208 B | 2542 ns, 8387 B | −30 %
|===

The client proxy looked its bean's scope annotation up by name (`Class.forName`) on every call
(BUG-20261028-01); the scope is now resolved once. The intercepted subclass read its interceptor
callbacks and its bridge `Method` on every call; they are read once per class, and the chain is kept per
bean instance. The intercepted call goes through one `InterceptorManager.intercept` call, calls the
target directly when the chain is empty, and no longer copies its arguments. A class emitted as bytecode
is created through a constructor handle looked up once instead of a reflective lookup per creation.
What an intercepted call still allocates, 96 B with one interceptor, is the invocation context, the
context handed to the interceptor and the argument array of the `@AroundInvoke` call.

=== The start

* *Extensions frozen at build* — with ten build compatible extensions frozen by `vidocq:generate`
  instead of run at start, the start of a Vidocq application is 30 to 40 ms shorter, about 8 % of
  425 ms, in the quiet series of BENCH-20261113-01 (2026-11-13, vauban#288).
* *Classes the start loads* — BENCH-20261118-01 (2026-11-18, vauban#347) counts the classes the start
  of the Cassini bench application loads, and how loops on the start path brought them down: see
  xref:internals.adoc#start-classes[Internals — classes the start loads].

[#what-did-not-move]
== What did not move

`qualifiedEvent` is `orders.select(literal).fire(event)` with a fresh `Event` per iteration, so its
qualifiers are converted on every measurement. BENCH-20260914-02 predicted that the generated
readers of vauban#70 would remove that conversion cost. *They did not*: at the end of that campaign
(BENCH-20260914-04) allocation had fallen 7 %, the time had not moved, and allocation was still 12 %
above the pre-#70 baseline. The generated reader makes the conversion cheaper, not free, and what is
left is in the dispatch rather than in the annotations. The latest run (BENCH-20261028-02) is faster,
for reasons not attributed to a change, and still allocates 13 % more than before vauban#70.
An `Event` injected once and fired many times pays the conversion once.

That prediction is recorded in BENCH.md as having failed, on purpose: a benchmark history is only
useful if it keeps the guesses that did not hold.

== Methodology

* *Harness*: JMH, in `vauban-bench` — a module of the reactor, not a published artefact.
* *Hardware*: Apple M5 Max, 18 cores (6 performance, 12 efficiency), 128 GB RAM; macOS 26.6.2 (arm64)
  for the September runs, macOS 27.0.1 for the October runs.
* *JVM*: Temurin 25+36-LTS, default flags.
* *Mode*: average time per operation, with `-prof gc` for allocation per operation.
* *Machine state*: the runs were made on a machine that was **not idle** — load average between 2.6
  and 5.2 where it was recorded. Each entry records its own, and only runs made under comparable load are
  compared. Changes of the same order as that difference, or as the error bars (under 3.5 % of each
  score), are read as flat rather than as gains.

[WARNING]
====
These are microbenchmarks of the container's own hot path, not of an application. They say how much
cheaper resolution became; they do not predict what a given application will gain, which depends
entirely on how much of its time it spends in the container.
====

== Reproducibility

[source,bash]
----
# The latest numbers above come from these commands, from the root of the monorepo
./mvnw -ntp -pl vidocq-components/vidocq-components-jakartaee/vidocq-components-jakartaee-core/vauban/vauban-bench -am clean package -DskipTests
cd vidocq-components/vidocq-components-jakartaee/vidocq-components-jakartaee-core/vauban
java -jar vauban-bench/target/benchmarks.jar QualifierResolutionBenchmark -f 3 -wi 5 -i 5 -w 2s -r 3s -prof gc
java -jar vauban-bench/target/benchmarks.jar GeneratedCodeBenchmark -f 3 -wi 5 -i 5 -w 2s -r 3s -prof gc
----

Compare against the entries in link:{repo-vidocq}/src/branch/main/vidocq-components/vidocq-components-jakartaee/vidocq-components-jakartaee-core/vauban/BENCH.md[BENCH.md], which carry
the raw JMH output, the commit each was measured at, and the machine's load at the time. The
convention for this repository is that *no performance number is published anywhere without a
corresponding entry there*.
